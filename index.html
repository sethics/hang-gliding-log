    Gliders

    These lists start empty on purpose. Assembly is the one thing you don't want a generic checklist for. Walk a setup with John or a Lookout instructor, write down what they actually do for that exact glider, and type it in here. Cross-check against the manufacturer's manual.

    Rebuilding the record

    You can't invent flights, but you can find evidence of the ones you made. Work the sources below, then log what each one proves — flagged as reconstructed, with the source named. An honest estimate you can defend is worth far more to an observer than a tidy number you can't.

      Notes to self

      What to ask for

      John Heiney

      Ask for dates first, counts second — dates are what he's likely to actually have, and flying days is a gate on its own. Specifically: which days you flew with him, roughly how many flights per day, the glider and size, the site, and whether he still has any of the footage with timestamps. His video files are dated evidence.

      Lookout Mountain Flight Park

      Schools keep student progress cards and tow logs, and Lookout runs a big enough operation that yours probably exists on paper. Ask the front office for a copy of your student record and any tow-log entries under your name and USHPA number, plus the date your H2 was signed off and by whom.

      Your own archive

      Lookout's booking confirmations are the strongest thread you have — one email per day you showed up, already dated, already in your inbox. Search Gmail for flylookout.com and sort oldest first. Photo and video EXIF gives exact dates and often times. WeTransfer emails date the footage. Card charges and lesson invoices date the rest. USHPA has your membership and rating dates.
